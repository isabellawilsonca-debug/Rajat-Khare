Who Is Rajat Khare?

Rajat Khare is an Indian-born venture capitalist and the founder of Boundary Holding, a Luxembourg-based investment firm focused on deep technology. His professional work centres on identifying emerging technologies with practical applications across industries, including artificial intelligence (AI), robotics, aerospace, cybersecurity, and medical technology.

With an investment perspective shaped by technological development and commercial potential, Khare supports companies working to turn advanced research and engineering into solutions for real-world challenges. Through Boundary Holding, he participates in the development of a global deep-tech ecosystem spanning Europe and other international markets.

Boundary Holding: A Platform for Deep-Tech Investment

Founded by Rajat Khare, Boundary Holding focuses on technology-driven companies with the potential to develop scalable products and enter international markets. The firm's investment approach centres on innovations that address industry needs, improve existing processes, and introduce new technological capabilities.

Its areas of interest include:

  • Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

    Technologies that support data analysis, automation, and intelligent decision-making.

  • Aerospace and Drones

    Unmanned systems and advanced technologies for industrial, commercial, and security applications.

  • Cybersecurity and Surveillance

    Solutions designed to strengthen digital security, situational awareness, and infrastructure protection.

  • HealthTech and MedTech

    Innovations that support medical diagnostics, healthcare delivery, and patient mobility.

  • Robotics and Automation

    Systems that improve efficiency, precision, and operational performance.

By supporting companies at different stages of development, Boundary Holding seeks to help promising technologies progress from development to wider commercial adoption.

Companies in Rajat Khare's Investment Portfolio

Rajat Khare's investment activities span several technology sectors. Companies associated with Boundary Holding's portfolio illustrate how deep-tech innovation can address challenges in security, industrial operations, and data analysis.

France
Counter-drone technology

CerbAir

CerbAir, a French counter-unmanned aircraft systems company, develops technologies to detect and counter unauthorized or potentially malicious drones. Its solutions address security requirements around sensitive locations and critical infrastructure.

The company represents the growing importance of advanced sensing and counter-drone technologies as unmanned systems become more accessible.

India
Drone-based solutions

Asteria Aerospace

India-based Asteria Aerospace develops drone technology for industrial and surveillance applications. Unmanned aerial systems can support activities such as monitoring, inspection, and data collection, helping organizations access information from locations that may be difficult to reach using conventional methods.

Netherlands
Economic intelligence and data analytics

Datenna

Datenna, a Netherlands-based company, focuses on China-related economic intelligence and data analytics. Its technology helps users examine complex information and understand connections between organizations, industries, and international business activity.

This type of analysis demonstrates the role of data-driven tools in strategic decision-making.

Singapore
AI-powered video analytics

XRVision

Singapore-based XRVision specializes in facial recognition, computer vision, and video analytics. Its technology is designed to extract useful insights from video data for security and enterprise applications.

The company's work highlights the growing use of AI in interpreting visual information and supporting real-time monitoring.

Czech Republic
Predictive maintenance

Neuron Soundware

Neuron Soundware, based in the Czech Republic, develops AI-based acoustic analysis solutions for predictive maintenance. By analysing sounds produced by machinery, such systems can help identify potential faults and support earlier maintenance decisions.

This approach can help industrial operators reduce unexpected downtime and improve equipment management.

Rajat Khare's Approach to Deep-Tech Investment

Deep-tech companies often face challenges that differ from those encountered by businesses built around conventional software models. They may require substantial research and development, specialised engineering, testing, regulatory approvals, and longer periods before reaching commercial scale.

Khare's investment focus reflects the importance of assessing both technological potential and the practical conditions required for market adoption. Important considerations include the strength of a company's technology, its ability to address a clear market need, its regulatory environment, and its potential to expand across borders.

Responsible development is also important in fields such as artificial intelligence, robotics, and surveillance. As these technologies become more capable, businesses must consider data protection, compliance, transparency, and the effects of deploying automated systems.

Emerging Technologies Shaping Future Investments

Several areas of technological development are creating new opportunities for deep-tech investors.

Agentic AI and intelligent automation

Attracting attention for their potential to perform multistep tasks, coordinate workflows, and support complex decision-making. Their practical value will depend on reliability, oversight, and appropriate safeguards.

Green technology

Innovations in renewable energy, resource efficiency, and infrastructure monitoring are helping address environmental and industrial challenges.

Autonomous systems and advanced robotics

Applications range from industrial inspection and logistics to aerial surveillance and mobility assistance. Progress in sensors, computer vision, and machine learning is expanding what these systems can accomplish in real-world environments.

For investors, these sectors require an understanding of both technical progress and the commercial realities of bringing new products to market.
